Waves

Energy on the move: from the ripple on a pond to the light in the room.

General Properties of Waves

A wave carries energy from one place to another without carrying the material with it. A cork on the sea bobs up and down while the pattern of crests travels past it.

Two numbers describe most waves: how often a crest passes (frequency) and how far apart the crests are (wavelength).

  • Wave speed: v = f × λ
  • Frequency is measured in hertz, 1 Hz = 1 crest per second

Sound

Sound is a pressure wave. A speaker cone pushes air together, then pulls it apart, and those squeezed and stretched patches travel outward as a longitudinal wave.

Pitch is frequency. Slide it up and the rings pack closer together because the wavelength shrinks.

  • Speed in air at 20 °C: about 343 m/s
  • Human hearing: about 20 Hz to 20 kHz
  • Concert A is 440 Hz, a wavelength of roughly 0.78 m

Electromagnetic Waves

An electric field and a magnetic field oscillate at right angles to each other and to the direction the wave travels. Each one keeps the other going, so no medium is needed. Light crosses empty space from the Sun this way.

Radio, microwaves, light and X-rays are the same kind of wave. Only the wavelength differs.

  • Speed in vacuum: c = 299,792,458 m/s
  • For every band: c = f × λ

Light

Visible light is the narrow slice of the electromagnetic spectrum that our eyes detect. Each color is a different wavelength.

Glass slows each wavelength by a slightly different amount, so a prism bends violet more than red and fans white light into a rainbow.

  • Red light: about 700 nm
  • Violet light: about 400 nm
  • A nanometre is one billionth of a metre
Red is bent the least and violet the most. A larger spread mimics a glass that disperses colors more strongly.
Speed of sound is for air at 20 °C. Speed of light is for a vacuum.
